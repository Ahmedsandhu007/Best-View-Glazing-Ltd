1. What the CMS is and where to find it

The CMS is the editing screen built into the website itself, at the site's address followed by /admin. Every piece of text and every picture on the site is a field in one of its sections, so nothing needs a developer.

  • Address: https://<the live domain>/admin (it works on the Vercel address too).
  • Sign-in: one shared password, set by the site owner in Vercel as ADMIN_PASSWORD. Ask the owner for it; it is not written anywhere in the CMS or the code.
  • A session lasts 12 hours, then you sign in again.
  • Each section opens with a small picture of the page showing where its fields appear, with numbered notes. Read it the first time you open a section.
  • Every text field shows a character counter such as 48/120. Over the limit, Save is refused with a message naming the field.
  • View site in the top bar opens the live site in a new tab. Sign out ends the session.

2. How a change reaches the live site

Saving never changes the live site. Only Publish does, and the site then rebuilds for a minute or two.

  • Save (top right and bottom of every section): stores the section as a draft. The live site does not change. Save as often as you like.
  • Unpublished changes bar (bottom of the screen): appears once anything is saved. It counts the drafts and names the sections they are in.
  • Publish: sends every saved draft to the live site in one go. You cannot publish one section and hold another back, so finish all open edits first. Allow one to two minutes for the rebuild, then refresh the page.
  • Discard: throws away every unpublished draft and returns the CMS to what is live. It asks for confirmation. There is no undo.
  • Drafts are shared. Two people editing at once see each other's saved drafts, and whoever presses Publish sends both. Agree who publishes.

4. SEO fields, and what the site does on its own

Titles, descriptions, slugs, headings and alt text are the fields to care about. Sitemap, canonicals, structured data and the admin's noindex are handled by the site and need no attention.

Page titles and meta descriptions

They live in SEO titles & descriptions, one pair per page. Service pages take their SEO title from each entry in Services; blog articles have their own SEO title and meta description at the bottom of each entry (empty = the article title and summary are used).

  • The site adds | Best View Glazing Ltd after every title except the home page's, so write titles without the company name. Aim for 50 to 60 characters before the suffix.
  • Meta descriptions: 140 to 160 characters, a plain sentence with the service and the area in it.
  • Site keywords in the same section feed the keywords meta tag only. They do not change rankings; keep the list short and honest.

Slugs are URLs

Every list entry has a slug set when it is created. It becomes the page address (/blog/<slug>, /services/<slug>, /areas/<slug>, /case-studies/<slug>). Changing a slug moves the page to a new address with no redirect from the old one, so never change the slug of a page that is live and indexed. Get it right when you create the entry: the title you type becomes the slug.

Headings

The banner title at the top of each page is the H1. Section titles and the ## lines inside an article are H2s. Do not type "H1" or "H2" into the text; the site sets them.

Alt text

Every picture field has a description field beside it. Describe what the photo shows in one plain phrase, for example Timber sash windows on a Victorian terrace in Islington. No keyword lists.

Internal links

In articles and write-ups, link with [words](/services/sash-window-repairs) style paths. Same-site paths start with /; outside links start with https:// and open in a new tab.

Automatic, nothing to do

sitemap.xml and robots.txt, canonical URLs, Open Graph and Twitter preview tags, structured data (business, each service, FAQs, breadcrumbs, each blog post as BlogPosting), and noindex on /admin.

5. Publishing a blog article, step by step

An article takes about ten minutes in the CMS once the text exists in Google Docs or Word.

  1. Open Blog articles in the left menu and press New. Type the final title when asked: it becomes the slug (the URL), so get it right here.
  2. Fill the fields: Category (short and consistent, for example Double glazing, Sash windows, Landlords), Date written out (19 November 2026), Reading time (5 min read), Summary (one or two sentences; shown on the card and under the banner).
  3. Add the picture: Upload a picture or paste an https:// image address, then write its description.
  4. Paste the article into the Article box straight from Google Docs or Word. Headings, bullet points, bold, italics and links are kept. Each line becomes one paragraph.
    • By hand: ## starts a heading, - a bullet, **bold**, *italic*, [words](link).
    • Pictures inside the article: put the cursor where it goes and press Insert image; it asks for a description.
    • Keep paragraphs under 1,500 characters; the box counts words and blocks under it and flags any paragraph over the limit.
  5. Fill SEO title and Meta description at the bottom if the title or summary are not what search results should show.
  6. Press Save. Open View site and check /blog/<slug> reads well on a phone.
  7. Press Publish in the bar at the bottom. Wait one to two minutes, then check the live address.

The home page shows the first three entries in the list, so keep the newest article at the top: use Move up on the list page. To unpublish an article, delete it from the list and press Publish.

6. Case studies, services, areas, testimonials, contact details

These lists work like the blog, with a few fields of their own.

Case studies. Category is a fixed choice (Sash windows, uPVC, Aluminium, Double glazing, Doors, Commercial) and doubles as the filter button on /case-studies. Summary goes on the card, Description at the top of the page, the optional Full write-up below it in the same format as an article. Results are up to three number-and-label pairs. The six shipped entries are samples: the Sample content tick keeps a notice on the page until it is unticked, so replace them with real jobs and untick. Only state figures the company can stand behind.

Services. Each entry is a card on the home page and its own page. Summary is the card text and the page banner line; Service page paragraphs is the body; Common faults is the ticked list. Form label must match one of the Service required choices in Enquiry form exactly, or the quote form on that page will not pre-select the service. Do not change the slugs of the 14 live service pages.

Areas we cover. Five regions, each with its districts. Adding a district adds a page at /areas/<district slug> the next time you publish; removing one removes the page. Region intros show on /areas.

Testimonials. Name, general location (Islington, North London), quote, stars out of 5. Tick Sample content on anything that is not a real review; the site then hides stars and shows a sample notice. Untick once real.

Contact details. Phone, WhatsApp number (digits with country code, for example 447700900123), the WhatsApp opening message, email, address lines, opening hours, the map (an address, or a pasted Google Maps embed link) and the floating WhatsApp button. Anything left empty is simply not shown; an empty WhatsApp number removes every WhatsApp button. The shipped phone, WhatsApp, email and map are placeholders and must be replaced before launch.

Enquiry form. The three dropdown lists. Keep Service required in step with the services' form labels.

7. Pictures

Upload the company's own photographs wherever one is asked for; the shipped pictures are stock placeholders.

  • Upload a picture takes JPG, PNG, WebP, AVIF or SVG up to 4 MB. Large photos are shrunk to 2,000 pixels on the long side and converted to WebP in the browser before upload, so a phone photo is fine as it is.
  • Or paste an https:// image address into the field. Only use addresses the company controls or has rights to.
  • Service cards are shown 16:10, case studies 4:3, blog cards 16:10 and article headers 16:9. Landscape photos with the subject in the middle crop best.
  • Fill the description beside every picture; it is the alt text search engines and screen readers use.
  • An uploaded picture appears on the live site only after Save and Publish. The small preview in the CMS shows it at once.
  • Uploads are stored in the site's public/uploads folder and kept forever; there is no media library to tidy, so name files sensibly before uploading (sash-window-islington.jpg, not IMG_4021.jpg).

8. Before you press Publish

Publish sends every saved draft at once, so run through this first.

  • Every section you touched is saved (no red error under a field, counters within their limits).
  • No slug of a live page was changed.
  • New articles and case studies have a picture, a description for it, a summary and a category.
  • SEO title and meta description are set for any new page, within 60 and 160 characters.
  • Links inside the text point at real pages (/services/..., /areas/...) and open correctly in the preview.
  • Nothing in the text promises what the company has not agreed to: no guarantees, response times, prices, accreditations or review scores.
  • Sample content is unticked only on entries that are genuinely real.
  • You checked the page on View site on a phone-sized window.
  • Nobody else has unpublished drafts they are not ready to send (the bar names the sections).
What not to do: do not edit the slug field on existing entries; do not delete the only entry in a list the home page relies on (trust strip, how it works, quotation factors); do not paste whole web pages as an article; do not put the company name in page titles (the site adds it).
