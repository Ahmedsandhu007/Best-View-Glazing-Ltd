Amazon sync (Keepa) {{ store.name }}

What this page does

The rows on the store's Amazon tab get their price and stock from Keepa, six times a day: the sync asks which seller could really sell the product today and at what price, and puts that on OnBuy. Keepa's numbers are noisy (Amazon's own "delayed" offer, a glitch price a third of the usual one), so the sync has guards - and a Keepa plan only has so many tokens. Here you see how the last syncs went, which prices they lowered most, how many rows the guards took out of stock, and you change the settings behind those rules. Looking is safe. Saving the settings changes which price the next sync believes and what counts as in stock, so it asks for the confirmation box.

How a price and a stock are decided

For each Amazon row, every sync

1. Keepa says which offers exist for the product: Amazon's own, a third-party seller's, or none.

2. Amazon sells it (in stock): its price counts. Amazon's own offer is delayed or on back-order: it is not a source, and Keepa's cheapest-offer price includes it, so the sync ignores that price (guard 1); when no other seller has it the row goes to stock 0.

3. A third-party seller has it (and the strict rule is off): the cheapest New offer counts, with the usual-level rule so a deal or voucher does not drag your price down.

4. A price below the guard's share of the usual level (default 40%) is read as a glitch: stock 0 until it recovers (guard 2) - unless a second look at the Buy Box finds a real seller at the usual price, which then becomes the source.

5. The OnBuy price is the source price plus the profit schedule and OnBuy's commission. The price follows the source down at every sync; a price you typed above the formula stays.

Keepa charges about a token per product plus a few for second looks; when a run uses the plan's tokens up it waits for the refill, and a run that waits too long ends with "out of tokens" and changes nothing.
