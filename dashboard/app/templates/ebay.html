eBay sync {{ store.name }}

What this page does

The store's eBay rows get their price, stock and delivery cost from eBay every hour: each run takes a batch of the rows that are due (oldest and riskiest first), checks them on eBay, and pushes what changed to OnBuy. eBay lets the store make only 5,000 calls a day, so the whole sheet comes round every day or two - and until a row's turn comes, an eBay seller who has run out can still sell on OnBuy. Here you see how the last runs went, how long the whole sheet really takes to come round, how much of eBay's daily allowance is left, and what the dead-row purge removed. It only reads.

How the sync picks its rows

Every hour, while eBay's daily allowance lasts

1. It reads the eBay tab and leaves out the rows that have no supplier link (they cannot be checked).

2. It sorts the rest by age and risk: in-stock rows that have not been looked at the longest come first; rows flagged for a content problem or a blocked brand come last.

3. It sizes the batch from the time since the last run (a late run gets a bigger batch, so a day's calls are used whenever the runs come) and cuts it if eBay's remaining allowance is smaller.

4. Each row in the batch is looked up on eBay (price, stock, delivery cost), priced with the profit schedule and the category's commission, and written to the sheet; changes are pushed to OnBuy.

5. The last step removes rows eBay says are gone (out of stock for good, or a blocked brand) from the sheet and from OnBuy, with a backup tab.

The Amazon rows are not part of this: they come from Keepa, see the Amazon page.
